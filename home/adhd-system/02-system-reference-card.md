# The System — Reference Card

*The card to glance at when unsure where something goes, or when tempted to add structure. Built July 2026.*

---

## Four containers (Todoist)

| Container | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Inbox** | Raw capture. Everything lands here. Five ugly words. |
| **Shelf** | Processed but not committed. A menu, not a promise. One project per area: Margie's, Grackle, Home. No further structure. |
| **This Week** | Three rocks, max. The only promises that exist. |
| **Waiting For** | `WF – person, thing, asked [date]`. Born the moment you send the ask. |

Plus **Mac** — the agenda list for therapy sessions.

**Todoist settings:** no labels, no filters, no due dates except hard external deadlines.

**Capture:** Wispr Flow, Todoist Ramble, Siri. Capture stays ugly. Refine at the sweep or on planes — never at the sink.

## Rocks vs. gravel

- **Rock** = needs a calendar block (~1 hr+), a written done-line, and a report-back at the next planning meeting. Can be a project's next action, a big task, or a joint decision. Three per week, max. **The block is the commitment.** Estimate the time, then schedule more.
- One rock is usually the week's shipped system (an SOP or process doc).
- Work inside an anchor (facility-block repairs, Friday blocks, gym, huddle routing) costs no rock slot.
- **Gravel** = everything small. Worked directly from the Shelf in gaps. Never moved to This Week. Uncounted, guilt-free.
- Want a gravel session? Block the hour, open a Shelf project, run top to bottom. Don't move tasks.
- **Gravel never eats a rock's block.** Punch-list work during a rock block = avoidance → Mac list.
- Gravel that won't die for weeks is a rock in disguise, an anchor checklist item, or dead. Monthly skim decides.

## Projects vs. tasks

- Project = more than one sitting. Lives on the Shelf as one line.
- Projects are never picked for the week — only their **next physical action** is.
- If a project needs a plan, the plan is a one-page doc in the SOP folder. Todoist links to it.

## The Scan (daily, 15–20 min, timeboxed)

- Same order: texts → voicemail → email → manager log → Airtable maintenance log.
- Only items since yesterday. Three verdicts: **reply now (<2 min) / capture to Todoist / dismiss.**
- **Scanning is not doing.** Anything bigger becomes a capture.
- Standard: **nothing unseen more than 24 hours.** Unread counts mean nothing.
- Missed a day? Next Scan covers two days. Never "catch up."
- Team knows the rhythm: log = answered within a day; urgent = call or text.
- Then sweep the Todoist Inbox (~10 min).

## Weekly anchors

**Friday is the collaborative day** with Becky, on the shared green work calendar (structure and planning, not personal commitments). Roughly:

| Time | Block |
|---|---|
| 7:00 | Cash delivery (Grackle) |
| 8:00 | Bank + caffeine run |
| 9:00–10:00 | Management meeting prep |
| ~10:00 | MKC group manager meeting — hand off routed items and the shipped system |
| 10:30–11:30 | Management meeting follow-up |
| 11:30–12:30 | Airtable review |
| **12:30–1:30** | **Review Week Plan — the planning meeting** |
| 1:30 | B&J walk / triage |
| 2:00–5:00 | Monthly rotating block (see below) |

- **Review Week Plan = the planning meeting.** Two halves: look back (report-backs, actuals vs. estimates), then commit forward for next week (merge lists, pick three, set done-lines, block the rocks on the calendar together). Nothing hits the shared calendar outside this meeting.
- It sits right after the manager meeting, so manager items route straight into next week's plan.
- **Monthly rotating block (Fri 2–5):** one theme per Friday —
  1. Sales forecasting
  2. Financial review (usually with Lou)
  3. Photo processing / marketing organization
  4. Event & music planning
- Work done inside these blocks is anchor work — it costs no rock slot.
- **Facility blocks (weekly, year-round):** maintenance and facility work, usually solo, sometimes with helpers. Scheduled at Friday planning for the coming week. Parts staged before the block starts. Each session leaves one piece of knowledge behind (a note, a photo, a label). Summer adds scheduled crew days with helpers.
- **Gym blocks:** ~2 hours round trip. Commit to 3 a week; days 4 and 5 are bonus, never debt. Placed at Friday planning, ideally when Becky is busy elsewhere, and preferably right before focused work, since exercise sharpens attention for an hour or two afterward. Gym is protected anchor time: it isn't the first thing bumped, and it isn't a hiding place from a rock.
- **Money hour (with Becky):** bills, payments, coding — together, one sitting.
- **Daily check-in:** informal.

## Recurring commitments

Never free-floating reminders or phone alarms. Every recurring obligation attaches to a standing meeting's checklist. Doesn't fit a meeting → add to the nearest one or question it.

## Areas (monthly skim)

Seven lines in one note: Margie's facilities / Grackle facilities / food truck / financial admin / HR-people / brand-design / home. One question per line: *"Anything quietly on fire that isn't on a list?"*

## Maintenance (monthly, 10 min, at one Friday planning meeting)

- Shelf items untouched 90 days: promote / rewrite as next action / kill / park.
- Waiting For items stuck 2+ weeks: escalate / take back / re-scope / kill.

## The rules

1. Three rocks a week. Estimate, then halve.
2. Commit to checkpoints, not finish times: "2 hours, then I report and we decide."
3. Decide what "good enough to call it done" looks like before you start.
4. Bad news travels at discovery. Flagging a slip is doing it right.
5. Swaps are announced ("Pulling X in, Y moves"), never discovered.
6. No new tools for 30 days; after that, only for systems that survived on paper.
7. Cheap, reversible decisions get two seconds.

## Why the system stays dumb

Business complexity lives in the external layer: SOPs, checklists, the team, Airtable. The personal layer is the cockpit — four containers, three promises. When something feels too complex for the system, don't make the system smarter; ask which external home the complexity belongs in.

**The system is done. The restaurants are the project.**
