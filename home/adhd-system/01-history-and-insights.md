## The core insights (these are the load-bearing ones)

1. **Activation is the broken stage.** Any system that needs Justin to start it alone will fail. Every recurring obligation has to attach to a standing appointment with another person, or run automatically.

2. **Setup is stimulating; maintenance isn't.** The pattern is: build a system, love building it, abandon it in the maintenance phase. Design for maintenance, not launch.

3. **Building can be avoidance.** "Automate my system" and "new Airtable base" are the disease presenting as the cure. New tooling only after a dumb version survives 30 days.

4. **Accountability must be peer-shaped.** Scheduled, relational, between equals (racquetball used to do this) works. Supervisory or evaluative structure triggers resistance and defensiveness — including when it comes from Becky. The deeper knot: Justin knows he needs more structure and accountability, but struggles to separate accountability from punishment. The working definition here: accountability = reporting what happened, as plain information, to someone who's on your side. A missed rock that gets reported is the system working. Shame, lectures, and "you said you would" are punishment — they're not part of the system, and when they show up, that's worth naming (and a topic for therapy).

5. **Becky can't be the external working memory.** It conflates the marriage with operational scaffolding and strains both. The planning meeting is co-authored between equals; she's formally relieved of enforcer duty.

6. **Commitments ≠ activity.** Lots gets done every week. Only three things are *promises*. Broken promises generate the shame; fewer promises, kept, break the cycle.

7. **Gravel is seductive.** Quick tasks feel productive and are the perfect hiding place from a rock you're avoiding. A week of twenty small wins and zero rocks is the classic pattern.

8. **Shame blocks entry.** A wall of unread is *why* inboxes get avoided. Bankruptcy isn't cosmetic — it removes the shame barrier so a daily ritual can exist.

9. **Degraded mode is still the plan.** Good enough and finished beats perfect and abandoned. Taking the shortcut on purpose is following the plan, not failing it. A collapsed week resumes at the next meeting.

10. **Losing panic as fuel feels like losing drive.** When deadline panic stops being the engine, things may feel flat for a while. That's expected, not failure.
